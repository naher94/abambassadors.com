  Summary
  In this engaging conversation, Dan Flannery, Chief Operating Officer of Loews Hotels & Co., reflects on his remarkable hospitality career, from his early days at Marriott to leadership roles at Ritz-Carlton and the launch of the EDITION brand. He discusses the challenges and rewards of transitioning into luxury hospitality, the importance of attracting young talent to operational roles, and the strategies that helped distinguish EDITION in a competitive market. Dan also explores how food and beverage can define a hotel's identity, why brand integrity matters as companies expand, and how strong relationships in sales and distribution drive performance. Looking ahead, he shares lessons on industry resilience, leadership, hard work, and the interpersonal skills essential for the next generation of hoteliers.
  
  Takeaways
  • The hotel industry requires a unique fit for those who thrive in operations.
  • Dan describes the three major learning chapters of his career: Marriott, where he learned the hotel business; Ritz-Carlton, where he learned luxury; and EDITION, where he learned lifestyle, design, and entrepreneurship
  • He discusses the difference between serving a Marriott guest and serving a true luxury traveler, using Simon Cooper’s point that operators must stop judging luxury through their own lens.
  • Dan shares how Marriott and Ian Schrager approached the creation of EDITION, including the idea that Marriott would provide the chassis while Ian controlled the voice, feel, and creative identity.
  • He explains the brand-building framework behind EDITION, including Ian’s insistence that creative work should not be done by committee and the four core values that emerged: Passion, Authenticity, Sophistication, Enchantment
  • Dan emphasizes the importance of brand identity in hospitality.
  • Creating a brand requires deep conversations and a clear vision.
  • The restaurant bar scene is essential for a hotel's personality. 
  • Dan outlines why hotel F&B works best when the operator gives up control of the brand voice to a strong external partner while keeping responsibility for execution, staffing, and operational discipline.
  • Maintaining brand integrity is crucial when entering new markets.
  • Strong relationships with groups and intermediaries are key to sales success.
  • The hospitality industry is resilient and bounces back from crises.
  • Consolidation in the industry raises concerns about brand distinctiveness.
  • The hospitality industry offers unique career advancement opportunities from entry-level to CEO.
  • Hard work and interpersonal skills are essential for success in hospitality.
